# Conscious-traveller workspace

## Product direction

AVES should connect discovery, trip planning, preparation and travel in one useful workspace. Keep the same headers, footers, map, bird guide, carousel patterns, typography and controls across destinations. Apply documented stewardship and supplier evidence, without implying B Corp certification or inventing impact scores.

## Simple map controls

A direct globe button toggles the satellite background on/off. A small neighbouring layers button opens the existing discreet, dismissible picker. Retain geographic scope, remembered camera, overlays and bird selections. Keep the real map useful on a phone, with clear keyboard labels and pressed state. Satellite is a background choice, not a separate trip, a bird-range filter or live conditions.

## Trip preparation checklist

Start with an explicitly saved per-trip checklist. Tasks can be added, edited, completed and reopened. Use practical categories: flights, accommodation, activities, transport/car rental, gear, documents and other preparations. Optional due dates and reminders can follow. A checked task means the traveller completed that preparation; it does not by itself prove a paid or confirmed booking.

Next, attach relevant record links and notes to a task and trip. Examples include flight tickets, reservation confirmations, accommodation arrangements, car-rental details, supplier conversations and gear purchases. Distinguish ideas, requests, provisional arrangements, confirmations and cancellations. Keep source/reference details and dates visible where useful.

## Documents and conversations

Add secure private uploads as a separate increment after the checklist and record model work well. Enforce trip ownership and access checks, appropriate file types and limits, deletion/retention behaviour, and a recoverable experience for failed uploads. Do not expose records through public maps, links, search, logs or examples. Avoid collecting passport or payment information by default.

Conversations can begin with traveller-added notes and selected references. Any later email, calendar or messaging import requires explicit opt-in and a clear choice of what to import. Do not read an inbox, contact a hotel, send a message, make a purchase or create a booking merely because a checklist item exists.

## Delivery sequence

1. Direct satellite toggle beside the existing layers picker.
2. Reviewed precise-place pilot and clear map markers for the visual journey.
3. Per-trip checklist with explicit save/reopen/edit and ownership checks.
4. Record links, dates, provider references and status labels.
5. Secure ticket/confirmation attachments and traveller-added conversation notes.
6. Optional reminders and selected imports, followed by user-tested mobile refinements.

Keep the map-led visual itinerary connected to the trip workspace. Start with useful, verified functionality and preserve older drafts at each increment. Do not treat a new checklist as confirmation of live availability, payment, travel access or launch readiness.

## Route reading and evidence

Use official birding, walking, transport and ecotourism guides to ground route planning. Selected dated blogs and first-hand trip reports can add season, pacing, logistics and lived experience. Record publisher, URL, publication/review date, geography and scope. Keep historical accounts separate from current official access, closures, availability and operator arrangements. Conflicts and stale references need review rather than silent merging.

Use reviewed structured facts to inform itinerary suggestions, with provenance retained. Offer a compact “Read before you go” section beside relevant routes when the original source adds useful context. Link and attribute, summarize briefly, and avoid copying entire itineraries or articles. Do not present an old sighting or personal recommendation as current access, guaranteed bird occurrence or verified environmental performance. Supplier stewardship claims require their own evidence.

## Conservation organisations and mobile usefulness

Keep the unexpanded phone map useful: prioritise geographic canvas height, selected-place visibility, a clear scale and straightforward layers/expansion controls. Keep long details dismissible and out of the map canvas. Verify real unclipped dimensions rather than only the outer card.

Add a sourced conservation and environmental organisation directory, with an optional map layer sharing the same records. Include organisation type, focus, operating region, official website, review date, and documented visitor, volunteer or support opportunities. Distinguish office locations from project areas and visitor sites. Include community-led and Indigenous-led organisations on their own published terms. Protect sensitive wildlife/project locations. Do not infer endorsement, charitable registration, environmental performance or B Corp certification from directory inclusion. Begin with a reviewed corridor pilot before widening coverage.

## Five photographs per subregion

Aim for at least five distinct, relevant landscape/place photographs in each subregion gallery across Colombia, Australia and New Zealand. Apply the same target to more detailed place galleries as they are added, including the four current South Island place contexts. A country-wide collection or a five-photo total shared across several subregions does not satisfy the target for each gallery.

Reuse the shared gallery controls, counters, enlargement and compact in-place credits. Preserve active photo, geographic selection, page scroll and return focus. Keep multiple photographs of one place grouped together and make its own photo count clear. Do not count bird portraits, alternate crops, duplicates, illustrations or unrelated neighbouring locations towards the landscape-photo target.

Each counted photograph needs a checked locality, useful alt text, creator, original source, explicit reuse permission/licence and any modification statement. Prefer complementary views of landscape, habitat, public access/arrival, local nature and visitor context where suitable. Do not imply current access, seasonal conditions, wildlife sightings or supplier endorsement from a photograph. Keep sensitive wildlife sites private.

Deliver one coherent subregion batch at a time, beginning with existing galleries and then extending the same component and data model to the other countries. Track reviewed gallery photographs separately from unreviewed repository candidates. Keep incomplete collections visible and useful while clearly retaining their real counts; never pad a gallery to meet a number.

## Country galleries connected to subregions

Every country overview should show a balanced selection of photographs from each of its subregions, using the same reviewed photo records as the regional galleries. Label each image with its subregion and place. Do not let regions with larger collections crowd out others. Preserve the five-photo target for each subregion; the country overview is a curated window into those collections, not a separate set of duplicate assets.

Selecting a region on the country map or in the gallery selector filters the shared place gallery to that region. Browsing a photo identifies/highlights its associated region on the existing map, without an unsolicited close-up or disruptive page jump. Offer a clear All regions return, retain the active bird/landscape mode, and preserve slide and scroll through enlargement and credits. Keep bird occurrence claims separate from landscape-photo locality.

## Private trip journal and community sharing

Extend the trip workspace into a day-and-place journal for photographs, audio recordings, receipts, notes and selected booking records. Associate records with the appropriate trip/day/place and preserve dates, captions and creator/source details. Uploads require a secure owner-only storage model, supported file types/limits, recoverable failures and clear deletion controls. Keep receipt, payment, identity and private conversation details out of public views by default. Remove precise wildlife/nest coordinates and unnecessary image location metadata from shared exports.

Prepare a shareable edition separately: travellers choose entries, audiences and visibility, preview the result and can revoke access. Public community sharing and invitations are later, explicit actions; do not silently publish private records or send invitations. Community content remains attributed and distinct from current official access guidance or verified environmental claims.

First example: prepare an owner-reviewed Colombia pilot journal from actual supplied trip materials, with unresolved dates/locations marked for confirmation. Draft an accompanying invitation for Peter to try the New Zealand planner. Confirm the recipient and selected journal contents before sharing. Do not fabricate a completed journal, memories, sightings, quotations, receipts or permissions from the product brief alone.
