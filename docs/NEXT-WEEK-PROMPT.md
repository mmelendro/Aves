### B. Equal Earth world destination map (AVES-003)

**Placement and purpose.** Replace the main mixed-country carousel in the global homepage hero with a recognisable full-world map. Keep headline/copy on the left and map on the right on roomy screens, with natural stacking on phones. Keep each country’s existing bird carousel in its card below. Do not repeat the mixed-country hero carousel alongside the map. The map helps visitors choose a destination and understand AVES’s biodiversity focus.

**Projection and geography.** Use the genuine **Equal Earth** projection, with consistent projection of country shapes, ecological boundaries, labels and targets. It preserves relative geographic areas and has a familiar world-map appearance; it does not preserve every shape or distance. Follow the [projection authors’ reference](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/1/321/2019/ica-abs-1-321-2019.html). Start north-up with a conventional world arrangement and fit the full world proportionally to its container. Keep New Zealand and other small/island countries visible, with labels that stay inside the frame. Do not stretch the world into the portrait country-card ratio, crop off continents, hand-sketch geography or substitute Mercator/Robinson under an Equal Earth label. No automatic spinning, panning or zooming is needed for the first release.

**Current destinations versus future ambition.** Only Colombia, Australia and New Zealand link to AVES destination pages now; preserve their actual status, including New Zealand’s “In development” label and existing enquiry-only approach. Long term, the map should support at least all recognised megadiverse countries **and** biodiversity hotspots, with room for other destinations. Future ecological coverage is an ambition, not a statement that tours or partnerships already exist. Other countries remain geographic context until their public destination is deliberately added. Do not generate placeholder destination pages, booking routes or new database eligibility from map data.

**Two distinct ecological concepts.** Keep country-level megadiversity classifications separate from regional biodiversity hotspots and from AVES destination availability. Use a dated, authoritative classification reference rather than treating these terms as synonyms. The commonly cited megadiverse-country list includes Colombia and Australia; New Zealand is part of AVES’s story as a recognised **biodiversity hotspot**, not by relabelling it a megadiverse country. References: [UNEP-WCMC Biodiversity A–Z](https://www.biodiversitya-z.org/content/megadiverse-countries) and [CEPF’s official hotspot table identifying New Zealand](https://www.cepf.net/resources/documents/settin-priorities-future-investment-eleventh-meeting-donor-council). A short sourced New Zealand hotspot note can accompany its existing destination label in the first release.

**Visitors must understand what and why.** Both ecological categories are required in the completed map, not a speculative extra. Provide a visible, short legend and clearly labelled context controls rather than hiding the explanation behind hover. Use concise plain-language definitions:
- **Megadiverse countries:** countries recognised for exceptionally rich biodiversity and high numbers of species found nowhere else; they carry particular importance for protecting that diversity.
- **Biodiversity hotspots:** regions rich in unique plant species that have also suffered major loss of native vegetation; conserving their remaining habitat is especially urgent. The technical definition uses endemic plants and habitat loss, not simply bird counts or a general claim that a place has lots of wildlife. Put formal criteria/source detail in an optional explanation, using [Conservation International’s definition](https://www.conservation.org/learning/biodiversity-hotspots?showpage=Biodiversity).
- **AVES destinations:** the places visitors can currently explore with AVES; only the existing three destination pages are active, with their actual launch status. This is a service-availability category, not a conservation classification.

Make both ecological categories easy to identify and compare, with restrained fills/boundary patterns, labels and a clear legend that do not rely on colour alone. Megadiverse countries and hotspots can overlap; they are not mutually exclusive choices. Hotspots may cover part of a country or cross national borders. Explain New Zealand as a biodiversity hotspot without implying it is on the megadiverse-country list. Keep AVES destination targets prominent and offer a simple way to declutter the ecological context. Explain why this matters for conservation and thoughtful travel without implying AVES tours, partnerships, conservation funding or proven positive impacts in every highlighted place. Check that a first-time visitor can distinguish the categories, find NZ’s context and open each actual destination on phone or desktop.

**Style and familiar behaviour.** Use the site’s emerald accents, pale mint/neutral surfaces, thin type treatment, rounded framing and restrained boundaries. Keep the world recognisable and destination links prominent. Use clear country names and a short legend, visible hover/focus/selected states, keyboard access and equivalent text links. Hover is optional: touch visitors must understand and use it immediately. Provide generous label/target hit areas for small countries without changing their actual outlines or moving them to false locations. Preserve country navigation across the independently deployed apps using the established full-page links. Avoid colour-only status differences, cluttered legends and fake controls. Explain only the distinctions useful to visitors.

**Lightweight, extensible source.** Reuse the existing destination catalogue for public links/status, mapped through stable country IDs to the world geography. Keep ecological classifications and hotspot geometry as separate, versioned reference data. Adding a country should be a small data/content change, not a map rewrite. Prefer a lightweight SVG with correctly precomputed Equal Earth paths where practical; do not add a paid map service, runtime API key, heavy map SDK or a new backend just for this map. Keep source/attribution and commercial reuse terms for map and ecological geometry traceable. Simplification must preserve recognisable countries and usable island targets.
