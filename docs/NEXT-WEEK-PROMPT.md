## Priority 3 — Equal Earth homepage world map (AVES-003a / AVES-003b)

Replace only the global hero's mixed-country carousel with a familiar full-world map. Keep headline/copy left and map right on roomy screens; stack naturally on phones. - Use genuine **Equal Earth**, consistently projecting world/country/ecological geometry and labels. Preserve aspect ratio, north-up orientation and a recognisable conventional world arrangement. Keep NZ/island countries visible; no clipped continents, portrait stretching, invented geography, substituted projection or unnecessary spinning/panning. [Projection authors' reference](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/1/321/2019/ica-abs-1-321-2019.html).
- Use restrained emerald/mint/neutral styling, clear boundaries and country labels, usable touch/keyboard targets and equivalent text links. No colour-only distinctions, hover-only instructions or crowded inaccessible legend. Preserve full-page links between independent apps.
- Current AVES destination links are Colombia, Australia and New Zealand, with truthful launch/planning status. Geographic/ecological context is distinct from service availability: do not invent tours, partners, bookings or placeholder country pages.
- The complete map includes **megadiverse countries and biodiversity hotspots**, with room for future destinations. Country-level classifications and regional hotspots can overlap; hotspot boundaries must follow ecological regions, including cross-border areas, rather than colouring entire countries as a substitute. NZ belongs in the hotspot narrative, not by calling it a megadiverse country. References: [UNEP-WCMC megadiverse-country reference](https://www.biodiversitya-z.org/content/megadiverse-countries), [CEPF NZ hotspot reference](https://www.cepf.net/resources/documents/settin-priorities-future-investment-eleventh-meeting-donor-council).
- Both categories require an immediately understandable legend and simple **what/why** explanations: megadiverse countries have exceptional biodiversity/endemic species; hotspots have many unique plant species and severe loss of native vegetation, making remaining habitat a conservation priority. Explain endemic in plain language, cite [Conservation International's definition](https://www.conservation.org/learning/biodiversity-hotspots?showpage=Biodiversity), and put technical detail behind an optional explanation. Do not imply conservation funding or proven AVES impacts without evidence.
- Make the two ecological categories easy to identify with restrained fills/boundary patterns, visible labelled controls, sources and a way to declutter, while keeping destination actions prominent. Ecological highlights do not imply extra bookable destinations.
- Reuse the destination catalogue and stable country IDs; keep versioned ecological reference data separate. Prefer lightweight, correctly precomputed Equal Earth SVG where practical. Verify map/data provenance and commercial reuse rights; no paid map service, runtime key, heavy SDK or new backend is required just for this map. Future countries should be small data/content additions.
