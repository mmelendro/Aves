# AVES landscape and wildlife design process

Owner-selected direction, 10 November 2026. Apply this to future country, region,
subregion and place treatments. It refines the AVES ecotourism brief: connect people
with places without suggesting access, sightings or ecological authority we do not
have. No new commercial or certification claim is introduced.

## Start with place, not decoration

Use Utzon's study of natural structure and context as a design reference, not a
literal architectural motif. His [Design Principles](https://www.sydneyoperahouse.com/sites/default/files/collaborodam_assets/utzon-design-principles.pdf)
discuss nature, organic structure, headlands and materials. Our application is to
let terrain, water and animal behaviour determine composition.

1. Inventory the actual route and selected-map IDs. Country, planning region and
   selected public place are distinct contexts. Do not create duplicate geographic
   entities or infer an entire region from one park. List absent subregion pages.
2. Record sources for terrain, water, species and behaviour. Prefer public park,
   scientific, museum or bird-conservation sources. Existing sourced AVES content
   is a starting point, not proof of a newly invented wildlife interaction.
3. Choose the scene before drawing. Ocean only where a coastal context is intended.
   Inland valleys get a river where supported; lakes/wetlands get sheltered water;
   forest/alpine interiors can have no water. A region with a coast can still use
   an inland focal landscape. No whales or surf in an inland scene.
4. Draw the major contour and short inner ridges first. Keep one main boundary,
   taper interior detail, avoid duplicated horizon lines and awkward crossing
   ribbons. Snow follows high terrain and reference conditions, never icing caps.
   Composite symbolism must not be described as a literal geographic view.
5. Add wildlife sparingly. Use locally supported species and plausible behaviour.
   A forest bird need not join a flock. Small loose groups, natural wing planes,
   modest perspective variation and coherent relative animal scale take priority
   over geometric symmetry. Never force a vortex, synchronized ring or decorative
   row. Terns, not falcons, support the NZ fish-feeding concept. [Tara reference](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/species/white-fronted-tern).
6. Review the actual vector at final CSS size. Raster concepts explore composition;
   they cannot establish precise alignment, anatomy, stroke width or reflow. Keep
   stronger terrain, uniformly finer animals, rounded joins, a clear wordmark and
   local-country bird identity. Centre AVES optically above focal terrain; keep
   navigation, native links, focus indicators, zoom and touch targets intact.
7. Validate and release one reviewable increment. Check desktop, narrow reflow,
   keyboard/menu/focus, navigation and sticky scroll, all affected route IDs,
   no overflow, a production build and hosted rendering. Record device limits.
   Do not substitute a generated mockup for browser evidence. Publish only an
   approved target and preserve rollback, branches and source provenance.

## What this iteration taught us

Avoid repeated image-only adjustments once composition is selected. The concept
generator repeatedly enlarged birds, imposed perfect rings, changed fin anatomy
and missed alignment. Use deterministic vector geometry for final implementation,
then inspect the real page. Compare alternatives at matching dimensions. State any
remaining mismatch directly instead of declaring an imperfect image exact.

The NZ source is a pilot, not a generic world landscape. The broader catalogue must
hold explicit terrain/water/wildlife choices. Unknown context fails to a quiet land
contour, never automatically to mountains, surf or whales. Do not add data/services,
paid generation, customer records, sensitive wildlife coordinates or sightings.

Apply stewardship through accurate representation, low page weight, accessible
controls, attribution, local context and respect for wildlife. [B Corp stewardship
reference](https://usca.bcorporation.net/putting-stewardship-to-work-tracking-esc-standards/)
is a values reference, not a certification or standards-compliance claim. Recheck
current standards before making any formal B Corp assessment.
